01 / THEMES

Questions and methods

The International Environmental Conservation Laboratory studies problems involving soil–water–plant systems in agricultural and rural regions in Japan and abroad. We seek ways to combine sustainable use of local resources with environmental conservation and rehabilitation, with the aim of strengthening the resilience of local agricultural and environmental systems. Soil and water conservation provides a common thread across this work.

We begin with problems observed in the field, such as soil erosion, water quality and resource use. After documenting site conditions and the properties of soil, water, plants and materials, we test how their interactions—and the activity of plants and microorganisms—change outcomes. Experiments, measurements, analyses and field trials provide the evidence.

USLE and WEPP support soil erosion assessment; HYDRUS helps examine water and solute movement in soil. GIS, remote sensing and UAV imagery are used where they suit the question and spatial scale. Alongside measured effects, we consider material availability, circular use of local resources and maintenance to assess whether a measure can be sustained locally. Here, resilience means the capacity of local environmental and production systems to maintain or recover their functions under stress.

SOIL

Soil erosion & degradation

How do rainfall, slope, soil structure and land use affect soil erosion and soil loss? Field observations, soil analyses and estimates of soil loss inform conservation measures suited to the site.

WATER

Quality & management

Research examines nonpoint pollution in river basins, agricultural water quality, plant-based treatment and water management. Work in Bangladesh and India has also addressed groundwater arsenic in relation to soils and plants. Pitcher irrigation is studied as a water-management option for drylands.

PLANTS

Restoration & response

How do plants and soil amendments affect water retention, salinity and nutrient movement? Work on charcoal-treated soils assessed both plant growth and nutrient leaching.

CONTROLLED ENVIRONMENT

Light & plant water use

How does the timing of red and blue LED light affect tomato growth and transpiration? Hydroponic experiments compare irradiation patterns and plant morphology.

02 / STUDIES & PRESENTATIONS

Study settings

Dryland field settings include Inner Mongolia, China, for soil water and conservation, and the Salton Sea watershed in Imperial County, California, for the relationship between agricultural land use and salinity (AGU 2018 presentation).
