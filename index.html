01 / RESEARCH

Research vision and questions

Our goal is to advance understanding of soil–water–plant interactions and establish approaches that sustain local resource use while maintaining or restoring the environmental and productive functions of agricultural land and rural areas. Soil and water conservation is the foundation of this work.

Field investigation, basic experiments, measurement and analysis are combined to assess resource and material properties and their interactions. Models such as USLE, WEPP and HYDRUS are used according to the research question. Previous studies have also used GIS and remote sensing to assess land use and watershed conditions. The main research areas are outlined below.

01

How can soil erosion be reduced?

Field assessments of surface runoff, soil loss and soil structure inform soil conservation practices suited to local conditions. Research includes site-specific soil conservation in Inner Mongolia, China, and other settings.

FIELD MEASUREMENT · SOIL ANALYSIS · SOIL CONSERVATION ASSESSMENT

02

How can agricultural water be managed and treated?

Research evaluates agricultural water quality and use, addressing vetiver-based water treatment, irrigation management and watershed water environments. Studies in Bangladesh and India have assessed the effects of arsenic contamination in soil–water–plant systems.

WATER QUALITY ASSESSMENT · IRRIGATION MANAGEMENT · PHYTOREMEDIATION

03

How can soil, water and plant functions be restored?

Research examines the recovery of degraded agricultural land through studies of salinization, soil amendments, soil moisture and plant water use. It also addresses rehabilitation of tsunami-affected farmland and measures to combat land degradation and desertification in drylands.

SOIL AMENDMENTS · SOIL MOISTURE · PLANT RESPONSE

02 / THE LAB

What students do

Students move between field, greenhouse or laboratory work and data analysis. Projects are built around measurements that can answer a defined question.

Observe & sample

Document site conditions and collect soil, water and vegetation samples.

Design & measure

Compare treatments in field or controlled studies; record soil moisture, water quality and plant response.

Analyze & communicate

Check data, interpret limits and present results in a thesis or research presentation.

Current student research directions

  • Soil amendments and crop growthField and pot experiments compare rice-derived by-products and recycled materials using soil moisture, growth and soil properties.
  • Soil water use in the fieldMonitoring soil moisture and water status under different crops to understand how plant cover changes water use.
  • Salinity and restorationPot studies compare amendments, soil electrical conductivity, plant growth and leachate under saline conditions.

05 / FACULTY

Kingshuk Roy ロイ キンシュック

Professor, Department of Environmental Science, College of Bioresource Sciences, Nihon University. He also holds a concurrent faculty appointment in the Graduate School of Bioresource Sciences, working with master’s and doctoral programs. His work draws on agricultural engineering and regional environmental engineering, with research in irrigation and drainage, soil and water conservation, land degradation, agricultural water quality, soil–water–plant interactions, environmental conservation and the resilience and rehabilitation of agricultural land and rural areas.

Studied irrigation and water management engineering at Bangladesh Agricultural University; completed a master’s in agricultural civil engineering at Yamaguchi University (1994) and a doctorate in production environmental science at the United Graduate School of Agricultural Sciences, Tottori University (1997). Professor at Nihon University since 2014.
