02 / APPROACH

Soil and water conservation

The laboratory investigates soil, water and plant resources in agricultural and rural environments. It first examines their individual properties, then tests how biological activity and interactions among resources and materials alter their effects. Field surveys, experiments, measurements and sample analyses connect observed problems with evidence for conservation and rehabilitation. Depending on the question, USLE and WEPP are used to estimate soil erosion, HYDRUS to examine soil water and solute transport, with the choice guided by the research question. Previous studies have also used GIS and remote sensing to examine land use and watershed conditions. The suitability of a measure also depends on locally available materials, maintenance and the needs of communities.

03 / RESEARCH THEMES

Research themes

Soil erosion and land protection

Mechanisms and prediction of soil erosion; cover materials and reused crop residues to reduce water erosion; plant-derived soil aggregation agents; and improved permeability of Okinawan red soils using waste materials.

Water quality and environmental remediation

Plant-based treatment of polluted water; investigation of groundwater arsenic and its effects across soil, water and plants; and the study of water quality in affected watersheds.

Water saving and dryland management

Pitcher irrigation, membrane covers and water-retaining materials for dryland cultivation; reduction of plant water demand through soil–water–plant interactions; and prevention and prediction of soil salinization.

Agricultural and rural environmental engineering

Evaluation of clay-pipe subsurface drainage, faster composting with fermentation aids, and a low-cost modular rooftop greening unit.

04 / FEATURED STUDIES

Featured studies

Plant-based water treatment

Experiments examined the potential of naturalized plants to remove contaminants from polluted water.

Pitcher irrigation

Porous earthenware vessels were investigated as a way to deliver water more efficiently in dryland farming.

Crop-residue reuse

Reused crop residues were examined as surface cover to limit the loss of topsoil by water erosion.

Soil–water–plant interactions

The laboratory tested approaches to managing plant water use through the movement and availability of water in the soil–water–plant system.

05 / FIELD REGIONS

Field and study regions

Bangladesh and India

Groundwater arsenic, effects on soil–water–plant systems, and plant-assisted remediation.

Inner Mongolia, China · dryland field site

Pitcher irrigation, membrane covers and water-retaining materials in dryland and desertification-related studies.

Sabah, Malaysia

Watershed water quality in areas affected by mangrove decline and shifting cultivation.

Mekong basin and Thailand

GIS and remote sensing for water resources and agricultural land conservation.

Imperial Valley, California · dryland field site

Spatial assessment of soil salinization in irrigated dryland agriculture; a separate AGU 2018 presentation examined agricultural land use and salinity in the Salton Sea watershed of Imperial County.

Okinawa, Thailand, Nepal and the Mekong basin

Satellite imagery for soil loss assessment; Okinawan red soil permeability research.
