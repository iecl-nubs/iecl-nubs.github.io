Floating vetiver for water treatment

How does water type affect the response to floating vetiver treatment?

Study design

Floating units treated natural surface water, synthetic eutrophic water and livestock wastewater. Water-quality changes were assessed separately.

What was observed

Total nitrogen declined by up to 60%, and suspended solids declined in all three water types. Phosphorus and COD responses depended on water type.

Interpretation

Treatment effects varied among water types. Use of biomass after polluted-water treatment would require safety assessment.

These water-quality findings are reported in the 2025 floating-treatment paper, available here.

02 / BIOMASS

↑ Top

Testing vetiver biomass as agricultural mulch

Can aboveground vetiver biomass help conserve moisture and limit soil loss in temperate Japan?

Study design

Four treatments were compared: surface vetiver straw, incorporated vetiver chips, surface rice straw and bare soil. An okra field study examined moisture, growth and weeds; a separate controlled water-flow experiment measured eroded sediment and surface runoff.

What was observed

Biomass-treated plots had higher soil water content than bare soil on several dates, and the vetiver straw plot had the highest cumulative okra yield. In the flow test, cumulative sediment was 48.5 g for vetiver straw versus 3,781 g in its corresponding bare section; rice straw was 4.93 g versus 1,738 g in its separate paired comparison.

Interpretation

Each field treatment occupied one plot. The yield and moisture differences cannot be attributed to mulch alone. The sediment numbers refer to a separate flow experiment and paired sections, not replicated field treatment effects.

Based on the revised CEIS 2026 manuscript; these results have not been published as a journal paper.

Leaf-surface treatments and LED irradiation

How do leaf-surface treatments and LED irradiation affect transpiration under their respective growing conditions?

Study design

Fibrous membrane coating, particle film application and LED irradiation are distinct interventions. Yoshida, Kitani, Roy and colleagues published a 2002 study of fibrous membrane coating; particle film with a water-holding material was presented by Roy and Zwieniecki in 2017. In a controlled greenhouse, Yurika Kato and Roy reported a 2013 study of tomato growth and transpiration under red and blue LEDs and sunlight. Later experiments examined LED timing patterns.

What MTI means

In the later work, MTI = (morphology-predicted − observed transpiration) / morphology-predicted transpiration. A positive value means less transpiration than predicted for plant morphology. Lower transpiration does not by itself establish higher water-use efficiency or reduced irrigation withdrawals.

Results and limits

The 2002 fibrous-membrane study found responses that differed by species and growth stage. The 2013 LED abstract reported a lower proportion of transpired water under LEDs than sunlight in its test conditions, while the trend in shoot elongation was broadly maintained. These findings cannot be transferred directly to particle film or to other LED schedules. MTI in the later unpublished analysis is an interpretive measure for the tested light treatments.

Evidence: 2002 journal article (fibrous membrane); 2013 published abstract (LED, Yurika Kato and Roy); 2017 conference presentation (particle film, Roy and Zwieniecki). The later MTI analysis is unpublished. IHC presentation.

04 / FRAMEWORK

↑ Top

Soil–water–plant functions and regional rehabilitation

Does improvement in a single measurement show that agricultural function has recovered?

The system

Soil governs infiltration, storage and nutrients; water moves solutes and supports plants; roots, transpiration and vegetation feed back on soil and water. Diagnosis begins with the process of degradation and the function affected.

Conserve or rehabilitate

Conservation aims to maintain still-functioning systems and prevent further decline. Rehabilitation seeks recovery where important functions have been impaired. Both can apply within one agricultural system.

How to evaluate

A change in EC or soil moisture alone does not establish recovery. Measurements should follow the intended function: infiltration, water storage, salt movement, crop response or other relevant outcomes over time. Management is reassessed for local soils, water resources, land use and the needs of the surrounding rural community. GIS and remote sensing can help identify spatial patterns, but an image alone cannot demonstrate functional recovery on the ground.

This framework draws on an unpublished integrative review; it does not present new field results.
