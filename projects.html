Floating vetiver for water treatment

How does water type affect the response to floating vetiver treatment?

Study design

Floating units were used to treat natural surface water, synthetic eutrophic water and livestock wastewater, comparing changes in water quality.

What was observed

Nitrogen and suspended solids decreased, while changes in phosphorus and organic matter differed among water types.

Interpretation

Plant-based treatment needs to be evaluated in relation to water quality and treatment conditions. Any subsequent use of the harvested biomass also requires assessment of accumulated substances and safety.

Testing vetiver biomass as agricultural mulch

Can aboveground vetiver biomass help conserve moisture and limit soil loss in temperate Japan?

Study design

Surface mulches of vetiver and rice straw, incorporated vetiver chips and bare soil were compared. An okra field trial examined soil moisture, plant growth and weeds; a separate controlled-flow experiment measured surface runoff and soil loss.

What was observed

Amended field plots tended to retain more soil moisture, while surface mulches reduced soil loss in the controlled-flow experiment.

Interpretation

Aboveground biomass is being examined as a local resource for soil and water conservation. The field observations depend on the study conditions; effectiveness must be assessed in relation to soil, weather and application method.

↑ Top

Leaf-surface treatments and LED irradiation

How do leaf-surface treatments and LED irradiation affect transpiration under their respective growing conditions?

Study design

Outdoor studies examine leaf-surface treatments using fibrous membranes and particle films, while controlled-environment studies examine red and blue LED irradiation. These distinct approaches explore the relationship between plant growth and transpiration.

Approach to evaluation

Transpiration predicted from plant morphology is compared with measured transpiration. An indicator is being examined to help distinguish growth-related changes from responses to irradiation conditions.

Results and limits

Responses to leaf-surface treatments differed by plant species and growth stage. LED experiments showed that irradiation conditions altered the relationship between growth and transpiration. Reduced transpiration is evaluated together with plant growth, rather than taken alone as evidence of water savings.

Related publications: 2002 journal article (fibrous membrane) · 2013 published abstract (LED, Yurika Kato and Roy) · 2017 conference presentation (particle film, Roy and Zwieniecki) · 2019 AGU presentation (light effects and water saving)

Soil–water–plant functions and regional rehabilitation

Does improvement in a single measurement show that agricultural function has recovered?

The system

We examine the individual properties of soil, water and plants and the interactions among them. Roots and microorganisms also influence soil and water conditions, so the functions and usefulness of resources are assessed when they are combined.

Conserve or rehabilitate

Field problems and degradation processes guide the search for approaches that combine sustainable use of local resources with environmental conservation and rehabilitation. Local conditions and existing practices inform measures that can be adopted in the communities concerned.

How to evaluate

Field surveys, basic experiments, analysis and measurement are combined to assess water retention, infiltration, substance transport and plant responses. Models such as USLE, WEPP and HYDRUS are applied according to the research objective and assessed against field measurements. Previous studies have also used GIS and remote sensing to examine land use and watershed conditions.
