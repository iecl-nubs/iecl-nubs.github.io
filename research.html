01 / THEMES

Questions and methods

The International Environmental Conservation Laboratory studies problems involving soil–water–plant systems in agricultural and rural regions in Japan and abroad. We seek ways to combine sustainable use of local resources with environmental conservation and rehabilitation, with the aim of strengthening the resilience of local agricultural and environmental systems. Soil and water conservation provides a common thread across this work.

We begin with problems observed in the field, such as soil erosion, water quality and resource use. After documenting site conditions and the properties of soil, water, plants and materials, we test how their interactions—and the activity of plants and microorganisms—change outcomes. Experiments, measurements, analyses and field trials provide the evidence.

USLE and WEPP support soil erosion assessment; HYDRUS helps examine water and solute movement in soil. Previous studies have also used GIS and remote sensing to examine land use and watershed conditions. Alongside measured effects, we consider material availability, circular use of local resources and maintenance to assess whether a measure can be sustained locally. Here, resilience means the capacity of local environmental and production systems to maintain or recover their functions under stress.

SOIL

Soil erosion & degradation

How do rainfall, slope, soil structure and land use affect soil erosion and soil loss? Field observations, soil analyses and estimates of soil loss inform conservation measures suited to the site.

WATER

Quality & management

Research examines nonpoint pollution in river basins, agricultural water quality, plant-based treatment and water management. Work in Bangladesh and India has also addressed groundwater arsenic in relation to soils and plants. Pitcher irrigation is studied as a water-management option for drylands.

PLANTS

Restoration & response

How do plants and soil amendments affect water retention, salinity and nutrient movement? Work on charcoal-treated soils assessed both plant growth and nutrient leaching.

CONTROLLED ENVIRONMENT

Light & plant water use

How does the timing of red and blue LED light affect tomato growth and transpiration? Hydroponic experiments compare irradiation patterns and plant morphology.

03 / DEVELOPMENT

From basic research to field investigation and technology development

Basic research on the mechanisms, prediction and control of water erosion underpins the programme. Faculty research and undergraduate and graduate research by former students have also addressed resource recycling, greening and soil and water measurement methods. Beyond the current undergraduate projects, this body of work provides a foundation for research addressing field problems as they arise.

Water erosion: mechanisms, prediction and control

Studies have examined how slope length and width, rainfall, crop cover, ridge configuration and soil structure influence soil loss. Basic experiments on erosion processes and assessments of soil-loss equations provide evidence for conservation measures.

Related article (Roy, Kusaka & Fukada, 1995) ↗

Crop residues for soil erosion control

To investigate locally appropriate approaches to red-soil runoff control in Okinawa, sugarcane residues were mixed with locally sampled Kunigami-Maji soil. Experiments examined changes in soil structure and permeability with treatment duration, evaluating the potential of local resources for soil improvement.

Related presentation (Roy, Uenishi & Yamamoto, 2006) ↗
Related article (Roy, 2007) ↗

Soil aggregation materials and agents

Studies have examined plant-derived soil aggregation materials and approaches that use particle aggregation to limit soil loss. Material properties and soil responses are evaluated together.

Shortening composting time

Research on woody materials has examined wood vinegar and waste molasses as fermentation aids to shorten composting time, supporting the recycling of local organic resources.

Related article (Sato & Roy, 2010) ↗

Low-cost rooftop greening units

Portable greening units using inexpensive and reused materials were constructed and assessed through soil moisture, temperature and plant growth measurements. The work examines affordable greening approaches, including water retention, drainage and maintenance.

Related presentation ↗

A simple instrument for soil phase measurement

Research has also addressed the development of a simple instrument for determining the proportions of solids, water and air in soil, extending the laboratory’s work to measurement methods for soil assessment.

Subsurface drainage and soil water management

Studies have examined clay-pipe subsurface drainage and materials for improving soil permeability, relating water management to locally available materials and soil and water properties.

Related article (Kusaka, Fukada, Ohno & Roy, 1993) ↗

Green manure for salt-affected soils in Thailand

Doctoral research examined the use of Sesbania rostrata as green manure in salt-affected soils in Thailand, investigating plant resources as part of agronomic approaches to soil management and crop production.
